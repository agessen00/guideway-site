How it works

How does Guideway work, step by step?

A person can tell their story at the front desk, at intake, on the funder's form and at the referral. Guideway asks once. Time and attention are what a person brings to each meeting, and one conversation lets them spend it on their own plan.

01 · The framework

One conversation

Sixteen areas of life, each on a five-level ladder the person places themselves on.

02 · The next step

A written next step

The next level, three steps to reach it and a date on each, ready before the meeting ends.

03 · The Guide

A Guide alongside

A Guide walks with the person, and the record follows them, so a handoff is never a restart.

The framework

Where does a person stand, in their own words?

Each area asks one question with five answers. The person picks the answer that fits. Nobody is scored, ranked or told what they are.

Housing

How would you best describe your current housing situation today?

Level 1Crisis · Immediate action needed“I do not have a steady place to live and am staying in a shelter, outside, or somewhere temporary.”
Level 2Vulnerable · A major barrier to work, learning, or stability“I have a place to stay, but I may have to leave soon or do not know how long I can stay.”
Level 3Dependent · Managing with support, still needs help to progress“I have a place to live, but I need help to keep it or find housing that meets my needs.”
Level 4Independent · Stable in this area“I have a steady place to live that meets my needs, and I am not at risk of losing it.”
Level 5Thriving · A strong foundation, exceeding expectations“I own my home or am building savings or other assets to help keep my housing stable for the future.”

Getting help, such as SNAP, does not place anyone on a lower level.

A level describes a situation, never a person

Not what's wrong with you, but what happened to you. Someone at Crisis in housing is in a hard situation; they are not a Crisis.

Why sixteen areas instead of one score?

A single score tells a Guide nothing about Monday. A plan starts where the person says the weight sits, in the area they name.

What is still in review?

Three parts of the framework wait on people qualified to check them. Each is marked for where it stands.

  • PlannedFeeling SafeA separate, private pathway, never a tile to browse. It needs a quick exit and no stored history on the device. A domestic violence advocate is reviewing it.
  • HeldLegal & Civic StabilityWritten, and held. No participant sees its question until a licensed attorney has read it through.
  • Planned988 in Mental HealthChoosing the first answer will show 988, the crisis line, before any plan appears.

How do stages differ from levels?

Levels describe where a person is in one area. Stages describe the kind of support on offer. People move between stages in either direction.

Stage 01

Stabilize

Help right away. No eligibility screen, no enrollment.

Stage 02

Connect

Program enrollment, where intake and documents live.

Stage 03

Build Skills

Courses, education and coaching.

Stage 04

Maintain

What a person keeps after active service ends.

The stages follow Maslow's order, not his queue. All sixteen areas are assessed together, so someone can build skills in one area while stabilizing in another.

The next step

What is the next step, and when?

A person places themselves on a level, and the way forward is already written. What the next level looks like, three steps to reach it, three actions under each, and a timeframe on every step.

Housing, from Crisis toward Vulnerable

Within 48 hours

Connect to emergency shelter

Identify two or three local shelters or transitional programs. Schedule an intake appointment. Arrange the way to get there.

Within 5 days

Document housing needs

Complete a housing assessment with a case manager. Gather ID, proof of income and residency. Begin the application.

Within 2 weeks

Develop a housing action plan

Set short-term and long-term housing goals. Identify income or benefits that support them. Revisit the plan weekly.

Why three steps, not thirty?

In a hard week, more options slow a person down. A few chosen steps, in order, move them forward.

Why is each step small?

Under pressure, people decide fast, so order and defaults matter more than choice. Hope is knowing a path exists and that you can walk it.

Why does a person, not software, lead the work?

People find help through someone they trust, not through a directory or a search box. So the list of local help lives in the software, not in one staff member's head, and the Guide brings what software cannot: the relationship that makes any of it usable. The Guide holds the map, not the wheel.

The shape of the work, a theme and not a scale

Someone guides you

Help arrives through a person, not a portal. In a hard month, the Guide carries what you cannot carry yet.

You guide yourself

The plan is yours. You pick the area, place yourself on it and take the step.

You build with others

Everyone leans on someone, and that is the point.

You guide someone else

The neighbor, the newcomer, the person who starts three months after you. Goodwill has run on this for more than a century.

Trust grows in small moments

An assessment is a conversation, not a data-collection event. Harder questions come later, once the early ones have proved safe to answer.

Why does social connection sit beside food and shelter?

Research on social ties (Putnam, Sampson) and on mobility (Chetty) links the people you can count on to health, safety and getting ahead. So Social Connection is one of the sixteen areas, beside food and shelter, not after them.

Progress

Guideway shows what moved, beside what was done

Case management records the work: notes, services, referrals. Guideway keeps that record and asks a second question about every person. What changed?

What was done?

Referrals sent, services delivered, notes written. Every system records this, and it matters. “Six services and three referrals” is a record of effort.

What changed?

A level moved. Something in the way came down. A goal was met, or is still ahead. Guideway shows this beside the activity, never instead of it.

The person's own view answers five questions

Where am I now?Sixteen areas, each on a level you chose yourself.
What matters most?You name the area that carries the weight this season. The plan starts there.
What is in my way?In your words, with the local help that answers it.
What is my next step?Already written: the next level, three steps, the first action with a date.
Did I move forward?Where you were, where you are, what moved. Progress you can see is progress you can own.

Traction describes movement, never the person

Nobody is ever told their life scores 42.

  • ↑Strong progress · movement up in several areas
  • ↑Progressing · movement up in at least one
  • →Stable · no change, which is sometimes the win
  • →No movement yet · goals past their date, or no change where change was planned
  • ↓Moved down · opens a new plan; nobody is marked down

Movement down is pink, never red. It is as visible as any gain, without making a hard month look like a failing grade.

The same areas, across a community

The sixteen areas add up by location, program or time period, so an organization sees the community it serves.

Illustrative sample. Invented distribution for one area, to show the shape of the view. Not a finding, and not Guideway data.

  • Crisis 14%
  • Vulnerable 23%
  • Dependent 28%
  • Independent 29%

That makes “transportation is the area most often still unsettled at these three locations” answerable. Whether moving up in one area predicts steady work needs a pilot and a comparison group, and neither exists yet.

Which numbers show where help is still needed?

Top goals missed, ranked, shows which goals need a better design or more local help. It never ranks people. A referral carries a reason code when a service could not be reached, so the record shows where help was out of reach.

What can Guideway say about results?

Guideway may report that participants reported improvement. It may not report that Guideway improved outcomes; that needs a comparison group it does not have. Some apparent decline is honesty, because people share more as trust grows.

Who sees what, from the same record?

Four people read one record, so nobody rebuilds it in a spreadsheet, and the person it describes is one of the four.

Participants

Where you are, what matters most and what is next, in your own words, on a view that is yours.

Guides and frontline staff

More time with people, less with forms. What is improving, which goals are past their date and who to reach out to today, in seconds, not months of notes.

Supervisors and program leaders

Movement by area, program and location, always beside the mix of people each Guide works with. The goals missed most, and where referrals do not reach a service.

Funders and boards

How many people were served, and whether their lives moved, while they are still in the program. One shared language across programs and funding streams.

What changes

What changes for your team on Monday?

Put the three steps together and the work takes a different shape, without a longer form or another login.

  • One conversation in place of four intakesTold once, held once, and carried to whoever picks up the work next.
  • A plan exists the moment the conversation ends720 actions are already written, three under each of 240 timed steps. Nobody drafts a plan from a blank page at 6pm.
  • Progress you can show while the person is still with youFederal job results arrive months after a person leaves the program. Guideway shows movement while they are still in front of you.
  • It runs on the local help you already haveYour own list, organized by area and stage, so the map is still there on an experienced Guide's day off.
  • It fits how your organization already worksYour own goals and follow-up timelines beside the written ones, resource links for each program, access by program and location, and exports when a funder or auditor asks. Guideway handles enrollment. Billing stays in your existing finance system.

About

Which research and teams shaped Guideway?

Guideway is built with Goodwill teams, whose practice since 1902 has been a hand up, not a handout. It has no outcome data yet. Each design choice below traces to people who studied this for decades.

  • Machteld Huber, BMJ, 2011Health is the ability to adapt and self-manage. Why the framework describes circumstances, not conditions.
  • Bruce Perry & Oprah Winfrey, What Happened to You?Change the question. Why a level describes a situation and never a person.
  • Sendhil Mullainathan & Eldar Shafir, ScarcityAttention is finite and precious, and every form uses some of it. Why one conversation, and never the same question twice.
  • Barry Schwartz, The Paradox of ChoiceA curated few serves people better than endless options. Why a level carries three steps, not thirty.
  • Chan Hellman, Hope RisingHope can be measured and taught. Why the page shows a path forward, not a snapshot.
  • Robert Putnam, Bowling Alone; Robert Sampson, Great American City; Raj Chetty, Opportunity InsightsWho you can count on shapes health, safety and mobility. Why Social Connection is one of the sixteen areas.
Eight more sources
  • Michael Marmot, Whitehall StudiesA sense of control shapes long-term health. Why the framework is a ladder the person places themselves on.
  • Abraham Maslow, A Theory of Human Motivation, 1943Needs have an order, but not a queue. Why the four stages follow that order and no area has to wait for another.
  • Erik Erikson, Childhood and SocietyPast a point, wellbeing grows from being someone others lean on. Why the arc does not end at independence.
  • Donna Hicks, DignityDignity is a need, not a nicety. Why the questions are direct but never demeaning, and no one is a case.
  • Daniel Kahneman, Thinking, Fast and SlowUnder pressure people decide fast. Why order, defaults and plain language come first.
  • Chip & Dan Heath, SwitchMake the change small and script the first move. Why every step carries a timeframe and a first action.
  • Danielle Allen, Talking to StrangersTrust is built in repeated, respectful encounters. Why harder questions come later.
  • john a. powell, Belonging without OtheringBelonging is a structural condition, not a program. Why social connection sits beside food and shelter.

Status, October 2026

Where Guideway is today

Guideway is early. Here is exactly where it stands.

Stage
Pre-pilot. The framework is written; the product is being prepared for a first site.
Evidence
None yet. No pilot results, no outcome data, no comparison group.
Content review
Sixteen areas, updated October 2026. Health, mental health, substance use and Feeling Safe material is under review by qualified professionals, including a domestic violence advocate. Legal & Civic Stability is held until a licensed attorney has read it.
Accessibility
Designed against WCAG 2.1 AA. No formal audit performed; no conformance claimed.
Platform and security
Built on Microsoft Power Platform, with sign-in through Microsoft Entra ID. Access is set by role, program and location. No third-party certification held.
Availability
Working with a small number of design partners. Not generally available.
