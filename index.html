The problem · Mullainathan, Ehrenreich

Attention is the scarcest thing in a hard month

Scarcity takes more than money. It takes bandwidth — the room to plan, remember, decide. Every form spends what is left.

Most people tell their story three or four times before anyone can help. Front desk, intake, the funder's form, the referral. Four funding streams, four definitions of progress, one life — and no single view of the person.

Guideway is built against that: one conversation, held once, and one picture of the whole person that every program can read.

Step 01 · The framework · Huber, Perry, Hicks

Where someone is, in their own words

Each area asks one question with five ordered answers. The person places themselves. Nobody is scored, ranked, or told what they are.

The fourteen areas

    Two samples

    Each area has its own question and its own ladder, written for that area. Two shown in full; the rest in a demo.

    Housing

    Circumstances, not character

    Not what's wrong with you — what happened to you. A level describes a situation, never a person. Someone at Level 1 in housing is in a hard situation; they are not a Level 1.

    Fourteen areas, not one number

    A single score tells a Guide nothing about Monday. A plan starts where the person says the weight sits, in the area they name.

    Stages are not levels · after Maslow

    Levels describe where a person is in one area of life. Stages describe the kind of support on offer. Separate axes — people move between stages in either direction.

    Stage 01

    Stabilize

    Crisis response. Immediate need, no eligibility screen, no enrollment.

    Stage 02

    Connect

    Program enrollment. Where eligibility lives — intake, documentation, reporting.

    Stage 03

    Build Skills

    Courses, education, coaching. Where measurable progress happens.

    Stage 04

    Maintain

    Sustaining supports. What a person keeps after active service ends.

    Maslow's order, not his prerequisite: all fourteen areas are assessed together, so someone can be building skills in one while stabilizing in another.

    Step 02 · The next step · Heath, Schwartz, Kahneman, Hellman

    Shrink the change to the next step

    Place yourself on a level and the way forward is already written — what the next level looks like, three steps to reach it, three actions under each. Every one with a timeframe.

    Within 48 hours

    Connect to emergency shelter

    Identify two or three local programs. Schedule an intake appointment. Arrange the way to get there.

    Within 5 days

    Document housing needs

    Complete a housing assessment. Gather ID, proof of income, residency. Start the application.

    Within 2 weeks

    Build a housing plan

    Set short and long-term goals. Identify income or benefits that support them. Revisit weekly.

    Why three, not thirty

    More options don't help someone who is overwhelmed. They stall them. A curated few beats a comprehensive list.

    Why the step is small

    Under pressure, people decide fast, so sequence and defaults matter more than choice. Hope is not a mood — it is knowing a path exists and that you can walk it.

    Step 03 · The Guide · Payne, Allen, Powell

    A guide, not a gate

    People find help through people — not directories, rarely search. Through someone they already trust who can name the right thing in one sentence.

    So the map lives in the software, not in one staff member's head. What the Guide brings is what software cannot: the relationship that makes any of it usable.

    The shape of the work

    The whole arc, in four sentences. A theme, not a scale — nobody is placed on it.

    Someone guides you

    Help arrives through a person, not a portal. In a hard month, the Guide carries what you cannot carry yet.

    You guide yourself

    The plan is yours. You pick the area, place yourself on it, and take the step. The Guide holds the map, never the wheel.

    You build with others

    Nobody is self-sufficient, and the goal was never to make them so.

    You guide someone else

    The neighbor, the newcomer, the person three months behind you. Goodwill has run on this for a century.

    Trust is built in small moments

    An assessment is a conversation, not a data-collection event. Hard questions come late, once the early ones have proved safe to answer.

    Belonging is structural

    Social ties predict health, safety and mobility more than income does — which is why social connection sits alongside food and shelter, not after them.

    Your own view · the five questions

    The person sees their own journey

    Guideway is built so the person can answer five questions about their own life — and see the answers change. Every screen, for every role, traces back to one of them.

    Where am I now?Fourteen areas, each on a level you chose yourself. What is stable and what needs attention, side by side.
    What matters most?You name the area that carries the weight this season. The plan starts there, not where a form says.
    What is getting in my way?The barrier in that area, in your words — and the resource or referral that answers it.
    What is my next step?Already written: the next level, three steps to reach it, the first action with a date.
    Did I move forward?Where you were, where you are, what moved. Progress you can see is progress you can own.

    What changes

    What changes on Monday

    Put the three moves together and the work takes a different shape. Not a longer form and a new login.

    • One conversation replaces four intakesTold once, held once, carried to whoever picks up the work next.
    • A plan exists the moment the conversation endsRoughly 630 timed steps are already written. Nobody drafts a plan from a blank page at 6pm.
    • Progress you can show before your funder can measure itFederal outcomes arrive after exit on a wage-record lag. Guideway shows movement while the person is still in front of you.
    • It runs on the resources you already haveYour local list, organized by area and stage, so the map survives one experienced Guide's day off.

    Traction

    Case management tracks the work. Guideway makes the progress visible.

    Most systems store activity well — notes, services, referrals, documents. Guideway is built to answer a different question first: did anything actually move?

    What did we do?

    Referrals sent, services delivered, notes written. Every system records this, and it matters — but it is a record of effort. “Six services and three referrals” does not say whether a life moved.

    What changed?

    A level moved. A barrier came down. A goal was met, or missed. Someone stalled. Guideway shows this beside the activity, never instead of it.

    The participant scorecard

    Fourteen areas, where someone was, where they are now, and what moved. Legible in seconds instead of six months of notes.

    Traction, not a life score

    Nobody should be told their life scores 42. Traction describes movement, never the person.

    • ↑Strong Progress — movement up in several areas
    • ↑Progressing — movement up in at least one
    • →Stable — no change, which is sometimes the win
    • ⚠Stalled — goals overdue, or no movement where movement was expected
    • ↓Needs Attention — movement down, which re-derives the plan rather than penalizing anyone

    Movement down is pink, never red — as visible as any gain, without making a hard month look like a failing grade.

    The same record, five ways

    • The participantTheir own fourteen areas, then and now. Progress you can see is progress you can own.
    • The GuideWhat is improving, what is stuck, which goals are overdue, and who needs attention today — in seconds, not months of notes.
    • The supervisorThe team at a glance: progressing, stalled, needing attention — always with the mix of people each Guide is carrying.
    • Program leadershipMovement by area, program and location. Which goals are missed most, and where referrals stop converting.
    • The board and fundersNot only how many people were served, but whether their lives moved — and which barrier the community could not clear.

    And across a community

    The same fourteen areas aggregate by location, program or time period — and an organization starts seeing the community it serves.

    Illustrative sample. Invented distribution, shown to demonstrate the shape of the view. Not a finding, and not Guideway data.

    • Critical 14%
    • Vulnerable 23%
    • Dependent 28%
    • Independent 29%
    • Thriving 6%

    That makes “transportation is the largest unresolved barrier at these three locations” answerable. Whether moving up in one area predicts staying employed needs a pilot and a comparison group, neither of which exists yet.

    The most useful number is the one that looks bad

    Top goals missed, ranked, tells an organization which goals are badly designed or unsupported locally — not which participants failed. Referrals carry a required reason code when a service could not be accessed, because without it the data reads as people failing.

    What Guideway can and cannot say

    Guideway may report that participants reported improvement. It may not report that Guideway improved outcomes — that needs a comparison group it does not have. And some apparent decline is honesty: people disclose more as trust builds.

    Who it's for

    Four people, one shared view

    The same record answers four different questions, so nobody rebuilds it in a spreadsheet — and the person it describes is one of the four.

    Participants

    Where you are, what matters most, what is next — in your own words, on a view that is yours. Progress you can see and say out loud.

    Guides and frontline staff

    More time with people, less with forms. One record, one conversation, and a next step that is already written before the meeting ends.

    Nonprofit leaders

    Your community's needs at a glance — across programs, sites and funding streams, in one shared language rather than four incompatible ones.

    Funders and boards

    Progress across a person's whole life, in program and in real time — while federal outcomes are still waiting on a quarterly wage-record lag.

    Where Guideway is today

    Guideway is early. If you are evaluating it, you should know how early.

    Stage
    Pre-pilot. The framework is written; the product is being prepared for a first site.
    Evidence
    None yet. No pilot results, no outcome data, no comparison group.
    Accessibility
    Designed against WCAG 2.1 AA. No formal audit performed; no conformance claimed.
    Security
    Microsoft Power Platform with Entra ID sign-in. No third-party certification held.
    Content review
    Health, mental health, substance use and safety material is under review by qualified professionals.
    Availability
    Working with a small number of design partners. Not generally available.
